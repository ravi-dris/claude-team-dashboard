Key Insights

✦ Stable — Not Improving in May

General hours are almost identical: 144.7 days (April) vs 146.3 days (May) — a marginal 1.1% increase. Unlike what you'd hope to see, May didn't show a meaningful reduction. The volume of non-billable time is holding steady across both months.

✦ Bench Time Well Managed

Bench hours are nearly eliminated — 1.1 days in April and 0.75 days in May. This is a strong sign that employees are not sitting idle. Everyone is either on a project or contributing to internal work.

✦ Meetings Dominate — 66% of General Time

Discussions and internal meetings account for ~66% of all general hours in both months (Apr: 98.4 days, May: 94.1 days). While some meetings are necessary, this proportion warrants reviewing which recurring ones can be shortened or replaced with async communication.

✦ Persistent High Submitters

Abhijit Nair (22 Apr → 19 May) and Kandhi Omprakash (19.4 Apr → 16.1 May) are consistently the top general-hour submitters across both months. Both are senior technical roles. A review of their project allocation is recommended.

✦ Sharp May Spikes — Needs Review

Aditi Baitule Deshpande jumped from 4.0 → 18.9 days and Suraj Singh from 2.9 → 12.7 days in May. These are the two biggest individual increases. Likely between projects — confirm their assignment status and ensure they're allocated to billable work in June.

✦ POC Work Growing

Software - POC hours grew from 23.4 days (April) to 33.4 days (May) — a 43% increase. This reflects active prototyping and exploration work. If these POCs convert to billable projects, it will be reflected positively in June's general hours.
