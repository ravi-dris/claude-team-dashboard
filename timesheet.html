Key Insights

✦ July Jumped 86% — Breaking a Three-Month Decline

General hours climbed to 209.3 days from 112.3 in June — a 97-day increase. April→June had been falling steadily (132.2 → 120.2 → 112.3), so July breaks a genuinely good run and sets a new high (574.0 days across four months). Entries rose from 424 to 754, headcount from 40 to 46. This is the month to explain, not the trend to project.

✦ Onboarding Is 37% of the Rise — Largely Expected

Employee Onboarding leapt 4.8 → 40.6 days, accounting for 37% of the entire increase. Almost all of it is three people ramping up — Nitin Jangid (18.7), Nandita Sharma (10.4) and Diksha Hazarika (8.6) = 93% of the category. Six people submitted for the first time in July (30.2 days, 14% of the month). New-joiner ramp-up is legitimate non-billable investment, not leakage — but it should fall away in August.

✦ Even Excluding Onboarding, July Rose 57%

Strip out onboarding entirely and July still runs 168.7 days vs 107.5 in June — up 57%. So new joiners explain barely a third of the jump. The rest sits with existing staff: meetings +44.8 days, KRA +12.5 and Bench +8.7. This is the part that needs a management answer.

✦ Bench and KRA Are Each Essentially One Person

Bench rose to 19.75 days — 100% of it is Bhushan Barbuddhe, his second consecutive heavy month after 12.4 in June. Separately, KRA hit 17.3 days, of which Brahmjyot Singh is 16.8 (97%). Two individuals account for 36.6 non-billable days — 17% of the entire month. These are the two most actionable conversations on this page.

✦ High Band Grew to 12 People = 73% of the Month

12 employees logged ≥5 general days (up from 7 in June), and together they account for 151.8 days — 73% of July. The steepest risers are Nitin Jangid (0.8 → 18.7), Brahmjyot Singh (5.1 → 21.5) and Bhushan Barbuddhe (12.4 → 20.1). Concentration is high, so a handful of reallocation decisions would move the total substantially.

✦ Meetings Share Has Halved Since April

Meetings rose sharply in absolute terms (51.1 → 95.9 days) but their share held at ~46%, versus 45% in June, 61% in May and 69% in April — so the meeting-discipline gain of the last three months was retained rather than extended. Software-POC eased to 27.5 days (from 30.4) and Field Visit fell to 1.5. No single meeting owner dominates: the largest is 9% of the category.
