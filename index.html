Key Insights
Adoption Health

AI is a daily work tool, not an experiment

The team sends 242 prompts every working day. Even on weekends — 157 conversations happened on Saturdays and Sundays, 10% of total volume — showing AI has embedded into how people work, not just office hours.

19 of 21 members were active in the last 30 days. Both team leads (Ravi and Ankit) are in the top 5 users, signalling cultural buy-in from the top down.
Usage Archetypes

Three distinct working styles have emerged

Deep collaborators — Ravi (16 msgs/session), Kashish (29), Ankit (11.5) — use AI for complex, iterative problem solving over hours.

Volume users — Aastha (302 convs), Sachin (257), Abhinav (112) — treat AI as an always-on daily assistant.

Silent coders — Abhijit (165K lines, 3 chats), Samarth (168K lines, 30 chats) — minimal chat but massive code output; they've replaced manual coding with AI.
Client Impact

AI is directly inside client deliverables

This is not internal experimentation — real client work is AI-assisted:

Services: CRISIL data migration, Landmark patient vitals, ADG census mapping, farmer Aadhaar procurement workflow.

Product: NASSCOM Foundation portal testing, Bajaj Auto V2→V3 migration, JSL dashboard, TRI NUO data pipeline.
Productivity ROI

Code generation grew 74% in a single month

Combined code lines: 715K (April) → 1.24M (May) — a 74% jump at 99.8% acceptance, meaning virtually every AI-written line went straight to production.

At 30 lines/developer-hour, the team's 2.06M lines = ~68,700 hours of equivalent manual coding — delivered in 63 days by 17 developers.
Team Comparison

Product analyses. Services builds.

Product drives 88% more conversations (1,189 vs 631) at 7.5 msgs/session — AI used for testing, SQL debugging, data analysis, stakeholder reporting.

Services generates 30% more code (1.16M vs 896K) at 5.3 msgs/session — AI used for migrations, API integrations, DevOps, and technical delivery.
